## Product bug found and fixed (test-first)

`blindpass-controller serve` claimed authority ownership through a function that **closed the shared authority pool** after the claim. Later authority writes then failed with `PoolClosed`; the owner treated that as an unanswerable epoch check and **fenced itself**. Visible effect: enrollment approval (which publishes broker trust to the authority) dropped the connection and fenced the record, so no authority-backed controller could enroll a node.

- Test first: `p06_rr_claimed_serving_owner_keeps_a_working_authority_pool` (`crates/blindpass-controller/tests/production_ownership.rs`, ignored, runs through the authority driver). It claims ownership of an active record, then reads and publishes broker trust and asserts the owner is not fenced. Red: `Unavailable` at the publish; green after the fix.
- Fix: `claim_ownership`/`OwnershipSession` moved from `main.rs` to `crates/blindpass-controller/src/ownership_session.rs`; the session now keeps the `Authority` open while serving and closes it only on error or during the `serve` drain (`session.close_authority()` after `quiesce`).
- Why the earlier tests missed it: they exercised `claim_ownership` and the store with their own pools, never the serving path followed by an authority write.

## Harness corrections made while building it (not product defects)

- A workload registration binds the policy version current at creation; setting the policy **after** creating the workload made the broker discard the grant (`binding_mismatch`, visible only as a `grant_rejected` audit event while the controller operation stayed `granted`). The harness sets the policy first. This P03 behavior is unchanged and unreviewed here; whether an operator should see it is an open question.
- The one-shot workload client treats `grant_unknown` as terminal, so the harness delivers the grant file only after the broker acknowledged the grant (`node_inbox.acked_at`), as the P03 helpers do.
- A control "fresh exchange" run ratchets the broker's pinned issuer epoch and makes the later relay `rebase_required` (designed); the lost-page case is therefore its own scenario (L1/L2) and the success path runs after a higher reservation.
